# Agora curriculum

This course develops practical sales and negotiation skills with ethical reasoning woven through the same situations. It adopts the actual Carpe/Objection app and learning pipeline. The previous fixed-question dashboard was superseded.

## Teaching contract

Every lesson has a specific action the learner should be able to take, a concise principle with at least two checks, contextual examples and a hook, varied harder retrieval, and a held-back application in a different situation. Optional fieldwork takes the principle back to a willing real conversation without collecting or grading private reflections. A fourth lesson in each unit examines limits and integration; the final roundup includes a substantive boundary case.

Four questions recur: What supports the claim? Can people choose meaningfully? Who bears the effects? Who will deliver or repair the commitment? These are authored ethical commitments. Correct answers depend on explicit scenario constraints; reasonable disagreements belong in open reflection and branching labs. Ethical choices are not promised to increase revenue.

The engine supports rule, hypo, argue, term, steps, pairs, sort, trap, and num records. Native terminology is a data contract: a rule can be a practice principle, not an absolute ethical law. Provenance is shown separately from exercise correctness. Only authored misconceptions become wrong tiles or repair options; a sound principle from another lesson is not automatically a wrong answer.

## Unit progression

### u01: Understand before you pitch

Find the need. Hear the person.

- **Find the real problem** — Ask and adapt before recommending a solution.
  - Use at work: In a willing conversation, ask someone to describe a frustrating task. Follow one detail they mention before offering an idea.
- **Show that you heard** — Reflect meaning accurately and update when corrected.
  - Use at work: With someone willing to discuss a small decision, summarize their main concern in your own words and ask whether you understood it.
- **Check fit and the decision** — Discover relevant requirements and decision participants without overreaching.
  - Use at work: For a small proposal you are preparing, list one requirement you still need to verify and one person whose input matters. Ask only for information relevant to that decision.
- **Discover without overreaching** — Gather enough relevant information to check fit while respecting confidentiality and a chosen boundary.
  - Use at work: Before a willing discovery conversation, write why each sensitive question matters and a less intrusive way to check the same condition.
### u02: Make value credible

Useful promises. Visible evidence.

- **Connect feature to outcome** — Explain how a capability might help a stated need without inventing a result.
  - Use at work: Choose one feature of something you offer or use. Write the problem it addresses, how it helps, and one outcome you cannot yet claim.
- **Say what the evidence supports** — Separate observations, estimates, and unsupported claims.
  - Use at work: Find one claim in a proposal you control. Identify its evidence and rewrite it if the wording implies more than you know.
- **Make the comparison fair** — Disclose material tradeoffs and compare options on relevant facts.
  - Use at work: Compare two realistic options for a small decision. Include one limitation of your preferred option and one strength of the other.
- **Turn uncertainty into a useful test** — Design a bounded evaluation that distinguishes the measured signal from the desired outcome and defines the next decision.
  - Use at work: For a low-stakes proposal, write one uncertainty, one observable measure, one limit, and the decision you would revisit after a small test.
### u03: Work through resistance

Clarify concerns. Make room for choice.

- **Understand the objection** — Clarify a concern and distinguish it from a decision to decline.
  - Use at work: Think of a recent proposal that met resistance. Write two different concerns the same words could express, then one respectful question that could distinguish them.
- **Trade terms thoughtfully** — Offer viable changes to scope, timing, or support with their consequences visible.
  - Use at work: For a fictional service you could offer, write a full-scope option and a smaller option. State exactly what changes and what stays included.
- **Make urgency honest** — Communicate a real constraint without manufacturing pressure.
  - Use at work: Review a deadline you plan to communicate. Write what makes it real and what happens if the other person does not act by then.
- **When the objection reveals no fit** — Distinguish a resolvable uncertainty from a confirmed mismatch and choose a proportionate response.
  - Use at work: For an offer you know, name one question a small trial could answer and one essential mismatch that the same trial could not solve.
### u04: Earn the next yes

Clear commitments. Respectful follow-through.

- **Ask for a clear decision** — Propose a proportionate next step with the material terms visible.
  - Use at work: Write a short next-step request for a real or fictional proposal. Include the action, the commitment it creates, and any important unanswered question.
- **Follow up with permission** — Agree a relevant follow-up and honor changes to that permission.
  - Use at work: For a conversation where follow-up is welcome, agree a useful time and purpose instead of assuming a generic reminder schedule.
- **Deliver and repair** — Track commitments and correct a mistaken promise while meaningful choices remain.
  - Use at work: Choose a small commitment you own. Write the owner, date, success check, and who you would contact if it became unlikely to happen.
- **Keep the agreement aligned** — Handle a material change after interest or acceptance by checking authority, dependencies, and renewed agreement.
  - Use at work: Write a short handoff for a small agreed task: owner, deliverable, date, dependencies, and who must approve a material change.
### u05: Before you bargain

Know the problem, your options, and your limits.

- **Behind the request** — Separate stated positions from interests you have checked.
  - Use at work: For a low-stakes request, ask what the requested change would make possible, then summarize the answer.
- **Your real plan B** — Distinguish an available alternative, a target, and a reservation point.
  - Use at work: Privately write one feasible alternative to an upcoming agreement and one fact you need to check about it.
- **Room for a deal?** — Find an agreement range when limits are known and acknowledge uncertainty when they are not.
  - Use at work: For an upcoming discussion, separate known constraints from estimates and list one useful question about the unknowns.
- **Recheck the whole deal** — Set a conditional reservation point and update it when a material term, alternative, or constraint changes.
  - Use at work: For a low-stakes negotiation, describe your fallback, target, and minimum acceptable complete package. Mark one changed fact that would make you recalculate.
### u06: Build a better offer

Trade across priorities and make choices meaningful.

- **Beyond one number** — Identify trades that meet different priorities without hiding costs or promising impossible work.
  - Use at work: Before your next low-stakes negotiation, list two terms besides price and check which one matters most to the other person.
- **Give the number a reason** — Choose when an opening offer is supported and respond to an anchor using relevant evidence.
  - Use at work: For a real proposal, find one comparable reference and note one difference that limits the comparison.
- **A real choice of packages** — Construct genuinely acceptable packages and evaluate a menu against the recipient's own needs.
  - Use at work: Sketch two offers you could genuinely deliver, then list what you would need to learn about the recipient before recommending either.
- **Stress-test the package** — Check whether a package remains genuine, comparable, and deliverable when cost, capacity, or an essential term changes.
  - Use at work: Take two possible offers and list their price, delivery cost, timing, workload, and key exclusions. Change one assumption and see whether you still value them equally.
### u07: Handle power responsibly

Be firm, stay curious, and notice who can choose.

- **Firm without a bluff** — State honest limits while protecting confidential bargaining information.
  - Use at work: Write one truthful sentence that protects a private detail and one sentence that clearly states a real constraint. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **The difficult conversation** — Separate observations from interpretations and open a conversation without pretending away responsibility.
  - Use at work: For a minor disagreement, privately rewrite one accusation as an observation, an impact, and a question. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Who gets a voice?** — Recognize power differences and use relevant fairness criteria without assuming fairness means equal division.
  - Use at work: For a proposed agreement, name one affected person who is not at the table and one practical way to learn their needs. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Pause, support, return** — Distinguish hard bargaining from a stated threat, use a support route, and define conditions for resuming.
  - Use at work: With a willing partner, compare a firm price limit and a threat. Discuss which support or pause you would choose and what would make return acceptable; do not rank either person’s courage.
### u08: Make agreements work

Plan for uncertainty, delivery, and a useful no.

- **Agree about uncertainty** — Recognize a clear contingent term and check the measurement, risk, and incentives it creates.
  - Use at work: Choose a low-stakes future uncertainty and describe what you would check and do under two possible outcomes. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **A yes you can deliver** — Turn an apparent agreement into clear responsibilities within each person's authority.
  - Use at work: Improve one ordinary commitment by naming who will do what, by when, and how you will check it is done. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **The useful no** — Recognize when to revise, pause, or leave a negotiation and evaluate the process separately from the outcome.
  - Use at work: After a low-stakes discussion, note one thing you learned, one promise to keep, and one preparation change for next time. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Check the whole package** — Combine a future trigger with total cost, authority, and capacity before treating it as an executable expansion.
  - Use at work: Draft two possible expansion plans for a fictional event: an automatic approved stage and a stage requiring a fresh decision. Explain who carries risk in each, and discuss which you would choose without assuming one is universally fair.
### u09: Persuade with care

Make the case. Leave room to choose.

- **Stories with room to think** — Distinguish a relevant emotional appeal from pressure that bypasses a considered choice.
  - Use at work: With a willing partner, tell a short product story and ask what claim they inferred. Correct any inference your evidence cannot support. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Proof with context** — Evaluate what testimonials, popularity, and endorsements can actually establish.
  - Use at work: Inspect a fictional or public testimonial and name one claim it supports and one claim it cannot establish. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Frame without hiding** — Present useful comparisons while preserving information needed for a considered decision.
  - Use at work: Show two fictional offers in a common format with total cost, scope, and one limitation each. Ask a partner which detail was easiest to miss. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **One offer across every channel** — Check the combined impression of a headline, comparison, and sales conversation; repair contradictions before reliance.
  - Use at work: Create a fictional offer in a headline, a short message, and a comparison row. Ask a partner what commitment they understand. Discuss both a concise version and a fuller version that preserve the material limits.
### u10: See the whole table

Notice who gains, who carries risk, and who is missing.

- **Who is not in the room?** — Identify affected people and test a proposal through more than one ethical lens.
  - Use at work: For a harmless everyday decision, list one person affected who is not making the choice. Ask what information you would need before speaking for them. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Fair by which rule?** — Compare fairness criteria without pretending that fairness always means an equal split.
  - Use at work: With a willing partner, compare two ways to allocate a fictional shared resource. Explain one reason for each without trying to label either person fair or unfair. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **A real chance to participate** — Recognize how power, time, information, and access affect meaningful participation.
  - Use at work: Rewrite a fictional invitation so that a person can request an accessible format or more explanation without having to disclose private details. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Decide under real scarcity** — Apply a stated allocation rule, surface its tradeoffs, and give affected people a feasible review route when capacity is limited.
  - Use at work: Allocate four fictional places among six fictional applicants using two defensible criteria. Compare who benefits and who waits; discuss a challenge that would justify reconsidering the chosen process without scoring anyone’s moral worth.
### u11: Keep humans accountable

Use the tools. Own the consequences.

- **A draft is not evidence** — Verify AI-assisted claims and preserve accountable human review.
  - Use at work: Review a fictional AI-written pitch. Underline every claim that would need outside evidence before you would send it. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Personal without intrusive** — Use relevant information for an understood purpose without exploiting sensitive inferences.
  - Use at work: Write two fictional outreach lines: one based on a stated business need and one based on an intrusive inference. Explain why you would send only the first. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Build a route to correction** — Connect incentives, decision ownership, and repair rather than relying on individual good intentions.
  - Use at work: For a fictional promise, write who owns delivery, who can authorize a change, and how the buyer can report a problem. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Follow the approved version** — Track which claim was verified, notice material changes after approval, and coordinate a new check before the old claim is reused.
  - Use at work: Create a fictional claim log with the claim, evidence, approver, and a condition that would trigger review. Discuss how much detail is proportionate; avoid recording real personal or confidential information.
### u12: Put it into practice

Change roles. Face the tradeoff. Choose a next step.

- **On both sides of the table** — Prepare a feasible agreement while understanding the other party’s constraints and freedom to decline.
  - Use at work: With a willing partner, roleplay a small fictional purchase, then switch roles. Name one assumption that changed after the switch. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Repair the agreement** — Respond to a consequential mistake with correction, feasible choices, and accountable follow-through.
  - Use at work: Write a fictional correction in three sentences: what was wrong, what is now known, and who will help choose the next step. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **Judgment, not a verdict** — Distinguish facts, constraints, and values; revise a decision when relevant information changes.
  - Use at work: After a low-stakes decision, write one fact, one value you prioritized, and one new fact that would make you reconsider. Keep any personal details private. Discuss a reasonable alternative and what fact or value would change your recommendation; do not rate anyone’s character.
- **The handover challenge** — Integrate claim evidence, stakeholder capacity, approval, and changing conditions into one reviewable pilot decision.
  - Use at work: Roleplay a fictional pilot handover with a willing partner. One person proposes proceeding, the other asks for a pause. Compare the evidence, affected people, and tradeoffs; write a justified choice plus the fact that would reverse it. No sale or personal score is required.

## Provenance and teaching decisions

- Original lessons preserve all 72 reviewed activities. Item IDs change from `u01l1-1` to `u01-l1-1`; `originalId` records the mapping. Lesson IDs remain `u01l1` through `u06l3`. The original objective, takeaway, fieldwork, correct option, explanation, transfer status, and source IDs are retained. Each three-option choice receives a distinct, authored fourth distractor; whole options are rotated to distribute key positions. Original match and sort sets receive meaningful extra rows to use the inherited five-pair and six-row modes.
- Added explicit rule cards make the principle visible before its practice. Element lists are sets (`ordered: false`); sequence items are explicitly taught practice routines, not universal conversational laws. Every rule has authored examples, wrong tiles, and two inline checks.
- New contrast cases change a material fact: a requested demonstration after sufficient discovery; an authorized shorter onboarding option; a verified integration; stronger but still bounded evidence; changed workload; an explicit refusal; a feasible full-scope concession; completed review before a deadline; specific booking authority; a narrow new records request after sales opt-out; an approved remedy; clarified interests; a newly verified fallback; a checked reduced scope; an essential deadline; a supported opening range; and explicit package acceptance.
- Fourth lessons add proportionate discovery and information boundaries; bounded pilot evaluation; uncertainty versus confirmed mismatch; change control and handoffs; conditional reservation points; and package valuation under changed cost/capacity assumptions. These are original teaching applications of the existing source register, not newly claimed experimental findings.
- S1/S2/S10/S11 remain together on the original follow-up-question activity and its lesson foundations. Question counts are not taught as a sales formula. The source register’s correction and reply remain necessary context; no disputed effect-size claim is introduced.
- Sales foundations use S3/S4/S8 for listening, relevant questions, truthful dealing, and decision boundaries; S5/S6 for claim/evidence and material information; S7 for fabricated pressure; S9 for changes and workable agreements. N1–N6/N9 support preparation, alternatives, conditional limits, priority trades, opening evidence, and packages. E1 supports considering affected people and choice. The exact instructional routines and best-next-step keys are authored practice judgments bounded by the stated facts.
- All numerical inputs are invented exercise data, not market benchmarks. Checked calculations include 10 × 6 = 60 minutes, $600 + $200 = $800, $5,000 − $4,500 = $500, $6,600 − $4,600 = $2,000, $80,000 + $4,000 = $84,000 in the salary-minus-commute model that explicitly ignores taxes and other deductions, and $6,600 − $5,100 = $1,500. Price-overlap endpoints are inclusive only where the exercise explicitly states acceptance at the boundary.
- No legal sufficiency or business-outcome guarantee is taught. A commitment can be unauthorized and still need repair; authority must be checked before reliance. A status update is an action the seller controls, while another person’s decision remains pending until confirmed. MESOs means the set of simultaneous equivalent offers, and equivalence always identifies whose valuation and which model.

# Agora native curriculum: units 07–12

Authored 2 November 2026. This is the migration and expansion record for `app/src/units/u07.js` through `u12.js`. Original reviewed JSON files remain unchanged. ## Data lineage and shape

Each unit is a literal JavaScript object, exported for Node and pushed to `AGORA_UNITS` in the browser. Each contains four lessons of seven semantic items: 28 per unit, 168 total. Original lesson identifiers, objectives, takeaways, and fieldwork are preserved. Fieldwork now also explicitly invites an ungraded alternative or revision discussion.

The first three lessons retain all four reviewed original items, with a preceding authored rule, a term, and an additional contrasting scenario. Original choice items become native `argue` or held-back `hypo` items with four parallel options and an explanation per option. Every new fourth option is an authored, situation-specific distractor. Match, sort, and order items become `pairs`, `sort`, and `steps`; the source meanings and reviewed answer conditions are retained. The rule is linked by ID to each case and precedes every transfer in its lesson.

For example, `u07l1-1` becomes `u07-l1-decision1`; `u07l1-2` becomes `u07-l1-sort2`. Added teaching is `u07-r-1`, vocabulary `u07-term-1`, and the new contrast `u07-h-contrast-1`. Equivalent patterns apply to all six units. No runtime helper generates course items. Build-time authoring produced literal objects.

The total contains 24 rules, 24 terms, 36 argues, 48 hypos, 11 pairs, 11 sorts, six steps, six traps, and two number items. Each rule has a meaningful element set, wrong build tile, worked examples, and two authored quick checks. Examples explicitly introduce the four ethical lenses, four allocation criteria, and fact/constraint/value distinctions before those are retrieved. Steps contain plausible wrong tiles and an application whose keyed step is the next action in the stated routine.
